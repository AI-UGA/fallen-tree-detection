Fallen Wood After Hurricane Helene, Measured from the Air

A UAV inventory of coarse woody debris
Dang Hoang, Rishab Subramaniyan, Owen Norman, Joey Hattan, Roger C. Lowe III
Code, data notes and live map

Background

Hurricane Helene (September 2024) downed large areas of Georgia forest. The fallen timber, called coarse woody debris, matters for three reasons: it feeds wildfire, it guides salvage, and it stores carbon. Foresters usually measure it along field transects on the ground. Transects give a fair site average, but they cannot show where the wood lies. A UAV map at centimeter resolution can, if every fallen piece is found, measured and mapped automatically.

Objectives

  1. Find and outline every fallen piece in drone images.
  2. Measure each piece and split full stems from fragments.
  3. Map wood volume on a grid land managers can use.

Conclusions

One UAV survey and instance segmentation produced a piece-by-piece inventory of 478.7 ha. Full stems are only a quarter of the pieces but hold most of the volume. Wood is spatially clustered in a few hot spots, so a quadrant map can show crews where to start salvage and fuel work. These volumes are image-based estimates and are not yet field-validated, and small pieces are under-detected. Next steps are LiDAR canopy height and a second site.
